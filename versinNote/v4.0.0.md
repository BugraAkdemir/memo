# Memo v4.0.0 — Release Notes

> August 23, 2026 · [Download](https://memo.bugradev.com)
> The 4.x era begins with something deceptively small that changes every single conversation: **Memo now knows what time it is.**

---

## Big Feature: Real Time Awareness

Until now, Memo had no clock. A "good morning ☀️" typed at 11 PM got answered as if the sun were up; coming back after three days felt like continuing a conversation from five minutes ago — because to the model, it *was* five minutes ago. Language models don't experience time unless you tell them about it, and nothing ever did.

Every system prompt now carries a small `[Time context]` block:

- **The current local time**, always — day of week included, so "what should I do this weekend?" and "remind me tomorrow" land on the right footing.
- **How long the conversation has been quiet** — but only once the silence actually matters (15+ minutes). Ordinary back-and-forth stays clean; return after two days and Memo naturally knows it's been two days, without you re-explaining.

It's one of those features where the best review is absence: nothing new to click, nothing to configure — replies simply stop being temporally wrong. Fully offline, like everything else in Memo: the clock it reads is your machine's own.

## Also in this release

- **Internal quality pass:** a structural audit of the whole Go backend (via a live code-knowledge graph) found and removed genuinely dead code, and a test that silently failed whenever the suite ran late at night (a calendar fixture crossing midnight) is now stable at any hour.
- **Docs hygiene:** GitHub's automatic contributor linking no longer credits an unrelated "Botfather" account on release pages — Telegram's real bot handle is now escaped everywhere it appears as plain text.

## What's next

The 4.x line continues from here — the next milestone targets letting Memo represent you in third-party WhatsApp conversations, alongside closing the backend's remaining language-consistency seam. As always: local-first, offline-capable, zero telemetry.
